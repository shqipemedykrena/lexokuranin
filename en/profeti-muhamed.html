The Messenger, the best example1–13

  1. 1What it means to take someone as an example

    The Prophet ﷺ is the best example a Muslim must follow in actions and sayings, and a source of guidance in all matters. The book says Muslims need his example greatly, especially today when doubts and desires turn people away from the path of Allah. Allah ordered His Prophet to follow the guidance of the earlier prophets (Quran 6:90), and Ibn Kathir explained that his nation should follow what he was ordered to do. Ibn Taymiyyah added that the stories of the Prophets strengthen believers when they are tested.2

    Who are the people mentioned

    Ibn Kathir
    Abu'l-Fida' Ismail ibn Kathir of Damascus (died 774 AH), a scholar of Quran commentary. His Tafsir al-Quran al-Azim is one of the most famous and respected commentaries.3
    Ibn Taymiyyah
    Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (661-728 AH), a great scholar of fiqh, hadith and Quran commentary. Scholars count him among the renewers (mujaddids) of Islam.4
  2. 2Following the example of earlier Prophets

    Allah ordered us to follow the examples of the Prophets, as He told His Prophet to take their guidance as an example (Quran 6:90). The book then lists the matters in which we should follow them, explained in the lessons that follow. These are their zeal in worshipping and obeying Allah, their constant remembrance of Allah and supplication to Him, their humility and tears when their Lord is mentioned, and their deep knowledge of Allah.5

  3. 3The Prophets' devotion to worship

    One of the most important ways to follow the Prophets is their zeal in worship. Allah praised some of them as people of strength and vision (Quran 38:45), which scholars explained as a strong will to worship and clear insight in religion. Other verses praise their prayer and zakah (Quran 14:40, 19:55, 21:73). The Prophet ﷺ was also very diligent in worship, even though his past and future sins were forgiven, and Allah ordered him to pray long at night (Quran 73:26, 17:79).6

  4. 4How the Prophets remembered Allaah and made supplication

    The Prophets remembered Allah at all times and kept calling on Him, showing their need and helplessness. The Quran tells how Job, Jonah and Zechariah called on their Lord and He answered them (Quran 21:83-84, 21:87-90). The Prophet ﷺ also prayed to Allah often, especially in hard times. Umar reported that on the day of Badr he faced the Qiblah and raised his hands so long that his cloak fell, until Abu Bakr put it back and told him Allah would surely keep His promise.7

    Who are the people mentioned

    Ayyub (Job)
    A Prophet mentioned in the Quran. When hardship touched him, he called on his Lord, and Allah removed his suffering and gave him back his family. Quran 21:83-84
    Yunus (Jonah)
    One of the messengers, called "the man of the fish" in the Quran. He went off in anger, called out to Allah from within the darkness, and Allah saved him from distress. Quran 21:87-88, 37:139
    Zakariya (Zechariah)
    A Prophet mentioned in the Quran. He asked his Lord not to leave him alone without an heir, and Allah gave him a son, Yahya (John). Quran 21:89-90, 6:85
    Umar ibn al-Khattab
    A great Companion, ranked second after Abu Bakr, and the second caliph. The Prophet ﷺ married his daughter Hafsah.8
    Abu Bakr al-Siddiq
    The closest Companion of the Prophet ﷺ, who accompanied him on the Hijrah and became the first caliph. He was the father of Aishah, the Prophet's ﷺ wife.8
  5. 5The Prophets' humility before their Lord

    The Prophets were humble before their Lord: when His verses were recited to them, they fell down in prostration and wept (Quran 19:58). As-Si'di explained that their hearts were moved by great hope and fear. Yusuf asked Allah to let him die as a Muslim and join the righteous (Quran 12:101). The Prophet ﷺ was the most God-fearing of people and often prayed: "O turner of hearts! Make my heart firm upon Your religion."9

    Who are the people mentioned

    Abd al-Rahman al-Sa'di
    Shaykh Abd al-Rahman al-Sa'di, a scholar of Quran commentary. His tafsir, Taysir al-Karim al-Rahman, is praised as a good, easy and trustworthy book.10
    Yusuf (Joseph)
    A Prophet, son of Prophet Yaqub (Jacob). The Quran tells his story in Surah Yusuf and quotes his prayer to Allah. Quran 12:4, 12:101
  6. 6The Prophets' knowledge of Allaah

    The Prophets knew Allah better than anyone, and this deep knowledge of His Names and Attributes gave them firm faith and fear of Him. The more a servant knows his Lord, the more he reveres, worships and loves Him. The Quran tells how Abraham, Jacob and Noah were given knowledge from Allah (Quran 19:43, 12:68, 9:62). Allah also told the Prophet ﷺ to ask Him for more knowledge (Quran 20:114), so we must follow his example.11

    Who are the people mentioned

    Ibrahim (Abraham)
    A Prophet whom the Quran calls a man of truth and a prophet. He invited his father to the right path, telling him that knowledge had come to him. Quran 19:41-43
    Yaqub (Jacob)
    A Prophet, father of Prophet Yusuf (Joseph). Allah says he was a possessor of knowledge because of what Allah had taught him. Quran 12:4, 12:68
    Nuh (Noah)
    A Prophet whom Allah sent to his people. He told them that he knew from Allah what they did not know. Quran 7:59, 7:62
  7. 7Why following the Prophets is an obligation

    Ibn al-Qayyim taught that there is no path to happiness and success in this world or the Hereafter except through the Messengers, and that their example is the scale by which our actions, sayings and manners are weighed. We need it more than a body needs its soul. The book adds that we follow the Prophet ﷺ because his life is the most perfect of lives, as Allah chose him over all humans, so a wise person should learn his guidance and biography.12

    Who are the people mentioned

    Ibn al-Qayyim
    A scholar and student of Ibn Taymiyyah who wrote many beneficial books. Among them is Zad al-Ma'ad, a very useful book on the life of the Prophet ﷺ.4
  8. 8Reason to follow the Prophet ﷺ: Allaah's command

    The first reason to follow the Prophet ﷺ is that Allah Himself ordered it. Allah says that the Messenger is an excellent example for anyone who hopes in Allah and the Last Day and remembers Allah often (Quran 33:21). Allah also warns those who go against the Prophet's order that a trial or a painful punishment may strike them (Quran 24:63).13

  9. 9Reason to follow the Prophet ﷺ: protected from faults

    We follow the Prophet ﷺ because Allah chose him to deliver His message and protected him from faults, so his life was free of them. When he made a mistake by using his own best judgment in a matter where no revelation had come, Allah did not approve it and corrected it. Such a person is worth following, and his life story and guidance are worth studying.14

  10. 10Reason to follow the Prophet ﷺ: the wisdom in his life

    Another reason to follow the Prophet ﷺ is that his life contained great wisdom. The book names the areas where this wisdom appears: matters of faith, manners and guidance, and his perseverance in calling people to Allah and in fighting evil. This wisdom is one of the reasons the book gives for why Muslims should take him as their example.14

  11. 11Following the Prophet ﷺ and the path to success

    The book states that we will never gain success and victory unless we follow the Prophet ﷺ in his sayings, actions and manners. It reminds the reader that Allah has made the Prophet's life story and manners an example for us. So following him is presented as the condition for success, not just something good to do.14

  12. 12An example for every situation in life

    Allah made the Prophet ﷺ an example for us to follow in all circumstances. The book lists the roles in which he can be followed: as a man, a husband, a brother, a Prophet, a ruler and a leader. So whatever position a Muslim is in, he can look to how the Prophet ﷺ acted in that role.14

  13. 13Why we need to study the Prophet's ﷺ life

    To follow the Prophet ﷺ properly, we must first learn how to follow him. The book says we must study the different aspects of his life, including the way he dealt with different people. Whoever reflects on his life finds that it contains every kind of good manners that righteous and noble people consider decent.14

How to follow his example14–32

  1. 14The Prophet ﷺ as an example in good manners

    Allah praised the Prophet ﷺ for his great moral character (Quran 68:4), and his manners were based on the Quran. He was never harsh or loud, did not shout in the market, and forgave instead of paying back wrong with wrong. Anas served him for nine years and was never asked why he did or did not do something. Once Anas delayed an errand, and the Prophet ﷺ came up behind him smiling and simply asked if he had gone yet.15

    Who are the people mentioned

    Anas ibn Malik
    A Companion who served the Prophet ﷺ at home and on journeys from the age of ten. He was twenty years old when the Prophet ﷺ died.16
  2. 15The Prophet ﷺ as an example in tolerance

    Allah says that by His mercy the Prophet ﷺ was gentle with people, and that if he had been harsh they would have left him (Quran 3:159). Anas reported that a Bedouin once pulled the Prophet's rough robe so hard that it left a mark on his neck, and then demanded some of Allah's money. The Prophet ﷺ looked at him, laughed, and ordered that he be given some.17

    Who are the people mentioned

    Anas ibn Malik
    A Companion who served the Prophet ﷺ at home and on journeys from the age of ten. He was twenty years old when the Prophet ﷺ died.16
  3. 16The Prophet ﷺ as an example in shyness

    The Prophet ﷺ was also an example in shyness and modesty. Abu Sa'id al-Khudri said that he was "shier than a veiled virgin girl." The book gives this as one more quality in which Muslims should follow the Prophet's example.18

    Who are the people mentioned

    Abu Sa'id al-Khudri
    A Companion of the Prophet ﷺ who narrated his hadiths, including the description of the Prophet's ﷺ shyness.19
  4. 17The Prophet ﷺ as an example in kindness and mercy

    Allah describes the Prophet ﷺ as a mercy to the worlds (Quran 21:107). A Companion reported that one night the Prophet ﷺ prayed until morning, repeating one verse about Allah punishing or forgiving His servants. He explained that he had asked his Lord to let him intercede for his nation, and this was granted for everyone who does not associate partners with Allah. When some young men stayed with him for twenty nights, he kindly sensed they missed their families and sent them home to teach them and lead them in prayer.20

  5. 18Loyalty to old relationships

    The Prophet ﷺ stayed loyal to the people he had loved in the past. Aishah said she was more jealous of Khadijah than of any other wife of the Prophet ﷺ, even though she never saw her, because he mentioned her so often. Whenever he slaughtered a sheep, he sent portions to Khadijah's women friends. When Aishah remarked on this, he praised Khadijah and said that from her he had children.21

    Who are the people mentioned

    Aishah
    Wife of the Prophet ﷺ and daughter of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq. She was the only virgin the Prophet ﷺ married.22
    Khadijah
    The first wife of the Prophet ﷺ. He married no other wife until she died, and she bore all his children except Ibrahim.22
  6. 19The Prophet ﷺ as an example in humility

    Allah ordered the Prophet ﷺ to be gentle with the believers who follow him (Quran 26:215), which the book explains as being humble and kind to poor and rich believers alike. He was so humble that he greeted children when passing them, and a young servant girl could take him by the hand and lead him wherever she wished. He mended his own sandals, patched his clothes, milked his animal, sat with the poor, walked with orphans and widows, visited the sick and accepted invitations, even from a servant.23

  7. 20The Prophet ﷺ as an example in bravery

    Ali ibn Abi Talib said that at the Battle of Badr the Companions sought protection with the Prophet ﷺ, who was the toughest of them and the closest to the enemy lines. Another Companion said the bravest men were those who could keep up with him when fighting grew hard. Anas reported that one night, when the people of Madinah were alarmed by a sound, the Prophet ﷺ had already ridden out ahead of them on a horse without a saddle, with a sword round his neck, and came back saying there was nothing to fear.24

    Who are the people mentioned

    Ali ibn Abi Talib
    Cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet ﷺ, known for his courage and for accepting Islam early. He married Fatimah, the daughter of the Prophet ﷺ.8
    Anas ibn Malik
    A Companion who served the Prophet ﷺ at home and on journeys from the age of ten. He was twenty years old when the Prophet ﷺ died.16
  8. 21The Prophet ﷺ as an example in generosity

    Ibn Abbas said the Prophet ﷺ was the most generous of people, and even more generous in Ramadan, when Jibril met him every night to study the Quran. Jabir ibn Abdullah said he was never asked for something and answered no. Anas reported that he once gave a man a flock of sheep filling a valley between two mountains. The man went back to his people and urged them to accept Islam, saying Muhammad gives like one who fears no poverty.25

    Who are the people mentioned

    Abdullah ibn Abbas
    Cousin of the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ prayed that Allah teach him the interpretation of the Quran, and he became known as "the scholar" and "the interpreter of the Quran".26
    Jibril (Gabriel)
    The angel who brings down Allah's revelation, called "the trustworthy spirit". He met the Prophet ﷺ every night in Ramadan to study the Quran with him.27
    Jabir ibn Abdullah
    A Companion of the Prophet ﷺ who narrated his hadiths. He reported that the Prophet ﷺ was never asked for something and said no.28
    Anas ibn Malik
    A Companion who served the Prophet ﷺ at home and on journeys from the age of ten. He was twenty years old when the Prophet ﷺ died.16
  9. 22The Prophet ﷺ as an example in fearing Allaah

    A Companion said he saw the Prophet ﷺ praying, and a sound like a grinding stone came from his chest because he was crying. Abu Bakr once said to him that his hair had turned grey. The Prophet ﷺ replied that what had made his hair grey were the chapters Hud, al-Waqi'ah, al-Mursalat, an-Naba' and at-Takwir. Both reports show how deeply he feared Allah.29

    Who are the people mentioned

    Abu Bakr al-Siddiq
    The closest Companion of the Prophet ﷺ, who accompanied him on the Hijrah and became the first caliph. He was the father of Aishah, the Prophet's ﷺ wife.8
  10. 23The Prophet ﷺ and worldly possessions

    Umar ibn al-Khattab once entered the Prophet's ﷺ house and found him lying on a simple mat that had left marks on his body, and Umar began to cry. He said he thought of Caesar and Chosroes sitting on thrones of gold, while the Messenger of Allah lay on this mat. The Prophet ﷺ asked him whether he was not satisfied that they have this world and we have the next. He also performed Hajj on a worn-out saddle cloth, in a garment worth less than four dirhams.30

    Who are the people mentioned

    Umar ibn al-Khattab
    A great Companion, ranked second after Abu Bakr, and the second caliph. The Prophet ﷺ married his daughter Hafsah.8
    Caesar (Heraclius)
    Heraclius, the king of Byzantium, to whom the Prophet ﷺ sent a letter calling him to Islam. Umar mentioned him sitting on a throne of gold, wearing silk.31
    Chosroes (Kisra)
    A king to whom the Prophet ﷺ wrote, calling him to Allah. Umar mentioned him, together with Caesar, sitting on a throne of gold, wearing silk.31
  11. 24Steadfastness and trust in Allaah's promise

    Someone asked al-Bara' ibn Azib whether they had fled and left the Prophet ﷺ at the Battle of Hunayn. He answered that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did not flee. He explained that the tribe of Hawazin were good archers, and he saw the Prophet ﷺ on his white mule, with a Companion holding its reins, calling out: "I am the Prophet in truth." The story shows his steadfastness and certainty in Allah's promise.32

    Who are the people mentioned

    al-Bara' ibn Azib
    A Companion of the Prophet ﷺ. He reported that at the Battle of Hunayn the Prophet ﷺ did not flee, and that no one was seen to be braver than him that day.33
  12. 25Patience with people and forgiving wrongdoers

    The Prophet ﷺ was patient with people and forgave those who wronged him. The book quotes a description of him: he was neither stiff nor harsh, and he did not raise his voice in the markets. He did not answer a bad deed with a bad deed, but he pardoned and forgave.34

  13. 26How the Prophet ﷺ repented and sought forgiveness

    The Prophet ﷺ set an example in repenting and asking Allah for forgiveness. Abu Hurayrah heard him swear by Allah and say: "I ask Allah for forgiveness and I repent each day more than seventy times." So the Prophet ﷺ himself turned to Allah in repentance many times every single day.35

    Who are the people mentioned

    Abu Hurayrah
    A Companion of the Prophet ﷺ. He said that no Companion narrated more hadiths from the Prophet ﷺ than he did, except Abdullah ibn Amr, who wrote them down.36
  14. 27How the Prophet ﷺ performed acts of worship

    Aishah said the Prophet ﷺ stood in night prayer until his feet swelled. When she asked why, since Allah had forgiven his past and future sins, he said: "Should I not be a thankful servant?" She also described a night when he prayed and wept until his beard and the ground were wet, and when Bilal came to call the dawn prayer, he explained that a verse had been revealed that night (Quran 3:190). In Ramadan he gave charity, recited the Quran, prayed, remembered Allah and made i'tikaf.37

    Who are the people mentioned

    Aishah
    Wife of the Prophet ﷺ and daughter of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq. She was the only virgin the Prophet ﷺ married.22
    Bilal
    A Companion whom the Prophet ﷺ chose to give the call to prayer (adhan) because of his beautiful voice.38
  15. 28How the Prophet ﷺ practised voluntary fasting

    The book describes how the Prophet ﷺ did voluntary fasting. Sometimes he fasted many days in a row until people thought he would not stop, and sometimes he went many days without fasting until people thought he would not fast. He never fasted a whole month except Ramadan, he fasted most in the month of Sha'ban, and he was keen to fast on Mondays and Thursdays.39

  16. 29How the Prophet ﷺ recited the Qur'aan

    The Prophet ﷺ recited the Quran slowly, one word and one verse at a time, and lengthened the vowels in words such as Ar-Rahman and Ar-Rahim. He sought refuge with Allah from the devil before starting to recite, and he read a set portion of the Quran every day without fail. He recited standing, sitting and lying down, with or without wudu, but not while in a state of janabah (major ritual impurity).40

  17. 30Prayer, fasting and marriage: following his balanced way

    Three men asked about the Prophet's ﷺ worship and felt their own was little. One vowed to pray all night, one to fast without a break, and one never to marry. The Prophet ﷺ told them he feared Allah the most, yet he fasted and broke his fast, prayed and slept, and married, and whoever turns away from his way is not from him. Ibn Hajar explained that his way was the simple way, and the Prophet ﷺ also told Muslims to take their Hajj rituals from him.41

    Who are the people mentioned

    Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani
    Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani (died 852 AH), a prominent scholar. He wrote Fath al-Bari, a commentary on Sahih al-Bukhari.4
  18. 31The Prophet's ﷺ daily habits: eating, sleeping and more

    The Prophet ﷺ never found fault with food: if he liked it he ate, otherwise he left it, and he said Bismillah before eating and praised Allah afterwards. He slept on his right side while remembering Allah, and on waking he used the siwak, made wudu and prayed. He spoke slowly and clearly, often repeating his words three times so people understood, and his laughter was mostly smiling. He also served his family, joked while always telling the truth, and visited the sick.42

  19. 32Which of the Prophet's ﷺ deeds must we follow?

    The book divides the Prophet's ﷺ deeds into four types. Deeds of ordinary human nature, like his way of walking or sleeping, are simply permissible, unless he kept doing them in a set way or encouraged them, such as sleeping on the right side. Customs of his people, like dress and long hair, are not rewarded acts in themselves, and some deeds were only for him, like having more than four wives. Acts of worship are what we must follow, and we also leave what he left, such as an adhan for the Eid prayer.43

His life in a few steps

  1. 570 or 571

    Born in Makkah

    He was born in Makkah in the Year of the Elephant, when the army of Abrahah came to destroy the Kaaba (Surah al-Fil). His father, Abdullah, died before he was born.533, 534, 535

  2. age 6 and 8 · around 576 and 578

    A young orphan

    At six he lost his mother, Aminah. His grandfather Abd al-Muttalib cared for him until he died, when Muhammad was eight. Then his uncle Abu Talib raised him.533, 536

  3. youth · around 579–594

    Al-Amin, the trustworthy

    As a young man he tended sheep for the people of Makkah and later traded on behalf of Khadijah. Because he was the most truthful and honest, his people called him al-Amin, the trustworthy.533, 537

  4. age 25 · around 594 or 595

    Marriage to Khadijah

    At 25 he married Khadijah, a wise and wealthy woman whose trade was run by men she employed. She reassured him after the first revelation and was the first woman to believe in him.533, 22, 537

  5. age 40 · around 608 or 609

    The first revelation

    At forty, in the cave of Hira, Jibril came to him, and the first words revealed were: "Read! In the name of your Lord Who created." The name of this site, Read the Quran, comes from this first word: iqra, which means "read".533, 538, 539

  6. 13 years · around 609–622

    The years in Makkah

    For thirteen years in Makkah he called people to worship Allah alone. The believers were persecuted for their faith, and he bore it with patience.533, 540

  7. the Hijrah · 622

    The move to Madinah

    He migrated to Madinah. Later, in the time of Umar, the Companions made the year of the Hijrah the start of the Islamic calendar.541, 538, 542

  8. 8 AH · 630

    Return to Makkah

    In 8 AH, after Quraysh broke the treaty, he marched with a great army and conquered Makkah. He pardoned those of Quraysh who had wronged the believers.533, 543, 544

  9. 10 and 11 AH · 632

    Farewell

    In 10 AH, in his Farewell Sermon, he told people: "Fear Allah concerning women", and set out their rights. He died on Monday, 12 Rabi al-Awwal 11 AH, and was buried in the house of Aishah.533, 545, 546

What he was like

Merciful

When he heard a child crying, he made the prayer shorter so the mother would not be worried. About kissing children he said: “Whoever does not show mercy will not be shown mercy.”547, 548

Humble

At home he served his family: he stitched his clothes, mended his shoes and milked the sheep. When it was time for prayer, he went out to pray.549, 550

Honest

Even before he became a prophet, his people called him al-Amin, which means “the trustworthy one”.533

An example for us

Allah says in the Quran that in the Messenger we have the best example. That is why Muslims try to follow him in what he did and what he said.551
