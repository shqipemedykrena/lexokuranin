The Meccan and Medinan buttons show only the surahs of that period. All shows all 114 surahs again.

Meccan surahs were revealed in the Makkah period, before the Hijrah. Medinan surahs were revealed in the Madinah period, after the Hijrah.1

Why? So you can see quickly which period a surah comes from. A common sign: Meccan surahs often say “O mankind”, Medinan surahs “O you who believe”. This is true most of the time, not always.2

What is a juz?

The Quran is divided into 30 parts of about the same length. Each part is called a juz. Each juz is split into two halves called hizb, 60 hizb in total.

This division does not come from the Prophet ﷺ. Scholars made it later, by the number of letters, to make reading easier for people. That is why a juz often starts in the middle of a surah.3

How are the juz used?

  1. To find your place. Click a juz below and the Quran opens where it starts.
  2. To split your reading. Read one juz a day and you finish the Quran in 30 days. The Prophet ﷺ told Abdullah ibn Amr: “Read the Quran in a month.”4
  3. To learn by heart. Some juz have a name, which makes learning easier: Juz Amma (the 30th, from surah 78) and Juz Tabarak (the 29th, from surah 67). People who learned the Quran by heart gave these names. They do not come from the Prophet ﷺ.5
