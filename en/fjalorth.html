Glossary

The Islamic words used on this site, with their meaning.

Allah

Allah is the One who is worshipped. He has all the attributes of divinity, and all of His creation are His servants.1

Basmalah (Bismillah)

Saying 'Bismillah' when starting anything means: I begin this action with the name of Allah, seeking help and blessing through His name.2

Dhikr (remembrance of Allah)

Dhikr (plural adhkar) is remembering Allah. It is liked and asked of everyone, and some of it is obligatory, such as some of the adhkar of the prayer.3

Du'a (supplication)

Du'a has two meanings. Du'a of asking means asking for what benefits or asking to be kept from harm, and du'a of worship means worshipping Allah with any type of worship.4

Hadith (narration)

A hadith is any report attributed to the Prophet ﷺ, even if he did it only once in his life and even if only one person narrated it from him.5

Hajj (pilgrimage)

Hajj means travelling to the Ka'bah, the sacred House of Allah, to perform the rites (manasik), that is, the actions and words reported from the Prophet ﷺ.6

Hijrah (emigration)

Hijrah (emigration) for the sake of Allah means moving from a land of shirk to a land of Islam.7

Iman (faith)

The people of the Sunnah agree that faith (iman) is words and deeds. It is saying it with the tongue, believing in the heart and acting with the body.8

Iqra' (Read)

The first revelation to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ came in the cave of Hira, when the angel came and said: 'Read!'9

Islam (submission)

The word Islam means submission and humbling oneself. It has become the name of the religion brought by the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.10

Jannah (Paradise)

Paradise is created and already exists. It is prepared for the pious (al-muttaqun), those who believe in Allah and His Messengers.11

Juz' (part)

Scholars divided the Quran into thirty sections, each called a juz' (plural ajza'). It is not known for certain who first made this division.12

Khatm (completing the Quran)

A khatm is completing the whole Quran, so repeating only a part is not a real khatm. The early generations would finish the whole Quran, then go back to the beginning and start again.13

Nabi and Rasul (Prophet and Messenger)

A Prophet (nabi) is one to whom Allah reveals something and who passes on what is revealed to him. If he is sent with it to people who go against Allah's commands, to convey a message from Allah to them, he is a Messenger (rasul).14

Quran

The Quran is the word of the Lord of the Worlds, revealed by Allah to His Messenger, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, to bring people out of darkness into light. It is the Book of Allah for all mankind.15

Ramadan and Sawm (fasting)

Fasting means refraining from things that break the fast, such as food, drink and intercourse, from true dawn until sunset, with the intention of fasting. Ramadan is a great month in which Allah revealed the Quran.16

Sahabah (Companions)

A Sahabi (Companion) is someone who saw the Prophet ﷺ, believed in him, and died as a believer.17

Salah (prayer)

Allah has made five prayers each day and night obligatory for every sane adult Muslim, performed in purity and humility, standing before his Lord. Prayer is a sincere turning to Allah alone in all one's affairs.16

Shirk (associating partners with Allah)

Shirk means giving Allah a partner or rival in His Lordship (rububiyyah), in worship, or in His names and attributes.18

Sujud (prostration)

Prostration (sujud) is a form of humility. A person prostrates on seven parts of the body, placing the forehead on the ground.19

Sujud at-Tilawah (prostration of recitation)

There are fifteen places in the Quran where the prostration of recitation is prescribed. It is recommended (mustahabb) for the reader and the listener, but it is not obligatory.20

Sunnah (prophetic way)

The Sunnah is the guidance of the Prophet ﷺ in all his affairs, meaning his path, his method and his way.5

Tafsir (exegesis)

Tafsir is the explanation (interpretation) of the word of Allah. Whoever does tafsir must follow its principles and guidelines.21

Tajweed (rules of recitation)

The rules of tajweed follow the way Arabs recite and pronounce words. Tajweed is part of speaking well and eloquently in Arabic.22

Tawhid (Oneness of Allah)

In Arabic, tawhid means declaring Allah to be One and Unique. In Islam it means believing in Allah alone as God and Lord, and giving Him alone all the attributes of Lordship and divinity.23

Ummah (community)

The word ummah has several meanings in the Quran. Its usual meaning is a group of people, a community or a nation.24

Wudu (ablution)

The pillars of wudu, without which it is not valid, are washing the face (including the mouth and nose), washing the arms up to the elbows, wiping the head and washing the feet up to the ankles. They are done in the proper order, without a long gap.25

Zakah (obligatory charity)

In Arabic, zakah means growth, blessing and purification. In Islam it means worshipping Allah by giving the kinds of zakah He has made obligatory.26
